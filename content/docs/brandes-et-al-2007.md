# A Statistical and Physical Description of Hydrometeor Distributions in Colorado Snowstorms Using a Video Disdrometer

**Citation:** Brandes, E. A., K. Ikeda, G. Zhang, M. Schönhuber, and R. M. Rasmussen (2007), A Statistical and Physical Description of Hydrometeor Distributions in Colorado Snowstorms Using a Video Disdrometer. J. Appl. Meteor. Climatol., 46, 634–650.

## In one sentence
Using ground-based two-dimensional video disdrometer (2DVD) data from Colorado snowstorms, the paper fits exponential and gamma size distributions, derives a snow density–size relation ρs = 0.178 D0^−0.922, and gives temperature, snowfall-rate and shape–slope (μ–Λ) relations for models and radar.

## Question
What form do particle size distributions (PSDs) take at the ground in winter storms, and how do their parameters relate to each other and to temperature, humidity and snowfall rate? What bulk density, aspect ratio and fall speed do the particles have? The authors state this is the first use of the 2DVD for winter storms.

## Data and method
- 2DVD: two orthogonal line-scan cameras, 10 cm × 10 cm area, ~0.15 mm resolution, at NCAR's Marshall site inside a double-fence wind shield with a Geonor gauge; winds < 4 m s⁻¹ only.
- October–April 2003–2005: 113 h from 52 storm days, mostly aggregates; ~80% of data warmer than −5°C.
- Exponential (3rd, 6th moments) and gamma (2nd, 4th, 6th moments) fits to 5-min spectra, truncated at 0.1 mm.
- Bulk density = gauge mass / 2DVD particle volume (silhouettes summed as "coin" slices).

## Main findings
- Gamma shape μ: snow mode +1 (near exponential), 22% negative (superexponential); rain mode 6, 4% negative (916 snow, 308 rain spectra).
- Density: ρs = 0.178 D0^−0.922 (g cm⁻³, D0 in mm; r = 0.82; 768 spectra), so m = 8.90 × 10⁻⁵ D0^2.1 g; closest to Holroyd (1971) and Fabry and Szyrmer (1999). Correlation with temperature (−0.14) and humidity (−0.12) is weak.
- Aggregate aspect ratio (vertical / horizontal maximum dimension) averages 0.9–1.0.
- Aggregates fall at ~0.8–1.1 m s⁻¹ (1–11 mm), within 0.1 m s⁻¹ of Locatelli and Hobbs (1974); mass-weighted fall speed rises from ~0.9 to 1.3 m s⁻¹ above −5°C, attributed to aggregation.
- Exponential fits: N0 = 7 × 10³ (T0 − T)^0.6, Λ = 2.27 (T0 − T)^0.18 (r = −0.63 for log N0, −0.41 for Λ); N0 is 3–5 times Houze et al. (1979) and falls ~4-fold above −5°C. Versus snowfall rate, N0 = 5 × 10³ S^−1.2.
- μ–Λ relations: snow μ = −0.00499Λ² + 0.798Λ − 0.666; rain −0.00325Λ² + 0.698Λ − 1.71; mixed phase −0.000120Λ² + 0.602Λ − 2.06.

## Why it matters
The relations serve bulk microphysics schemes that diagnose PSD parameters from temperature or precipitation rate (Reisner et al. 1998; Thompson et al. 2004) and polarimetric radar retrievals. A μ–Λ relation reduces the gamma PSD to two free parameters without fixing μ. The authors suggest model N0–T relations based on Pacific Northwest aircraft data may need adjustment.

## Notes for commentary
- Small particles are undersampled, so sizes below ~0.4 mm are suspect; Eq. (7) does not apply for D0 < 1 mm, and density estimates are called approximate.
- μ–Λ relations can arise partly from correlated moment-estimation errors (Chandrasekar and Bringi 1987; Zhang et al. 2003).
- Negative μ makes NT ill-defined; the authors suggest schemes predicting mixing ratio and reflectivity rather than NT. Habits were not discriminated.
