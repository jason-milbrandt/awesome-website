Hail Defense

A fixed-shooter arcade game about hail. Break up the formation of hydrometeors with radar pulses before it reaches the houses.

Hail Defense needs a browser with canvas support and JavaScript enabled.

How to play

  • Move: Left and Right arrow keys, or A and D. On a touch screen, use the arrow buttons.
  • Fire a radar pulse: Space, or the Fire button. One pulse can be in the air at a time.
  • Pause: P or Escape. Sound: on by default; M or the Sound button turns it off and on (the choice is remembered).
  • You have three trucks. The formation moves faster as it thins and with each wave. The game ends if the formation reaches the houses.
  • The houses absorb hits, and lose material with each one, from both sides.

Targets

Snowflake – 10 points
An ice crystal grown by vapour deposition. Its six-fold symmetry follows from the hexagonal lattice of ice.
Graupel – 20 points
A snow crystal or frozen drop covered in rime: supercooled droplets that froze on contact. Typically 2–5 mm across.
Small hail – 30 points
Ice of 5 mm or more in diameter, usually grown around a graupel or frozen-drop embryo.
Large hail – 40 points
Hailstones of several centimetres grow in strong updrafts. Alternating clear and opaque layers record changes between wet and dry growth.
Research aircraft – 50 to 300 points
Twin-engine turboprops fitted with cloud probes measure cloud microphysics in situ. One crosses the top of the screen from time to time.
Falling projectiles
Freezing rain (supercooled drops) and ice pellets (drops that froze while falling through a subfreezing layer).
